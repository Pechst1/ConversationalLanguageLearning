# Tentpole 4 · «La photographie»

*The reversal. The party held to save Le Mistral goes wrong, and it is your discovery that does it: one of your grandmother's Polaroids shows the fire starting in her kitchen. The learner came as the café's ally. By the last panel they are the heir to the problem.*

**How to read this script.** **A2** is the default line. **B1** is given only where it differs. Lines tagged *(silence)* have no dialogue. Character lines are captions under the art. **Your line** is drawn as a balloon inside its panel, with Toi seen from behind or cropped, never face-on. Notes marked **Note** are for the owner and are never shown to the learner.

---

## State in

- The group is holding the party: «Les 25 ans du Mistral». Margaux took over the café in 2001. The party is also a fundraiser, a *cagnotte* for Margaux's lawyer. Marchand refuses to renew her lease «pour faute»: according to him, the café's old wiring burned his building in 2022.
- **«Le mur d'Odile».** You agreed to show your grandmother's Polaroids at the party, sixty of them pinned above the booth. Lila copied the handwriting on the back of each one onto a small card. Nobody has read every back: some of the Polaroids are stuck together.
- **Flags read:**
  - `s1.promised_to` decides which announcement the scene interrupts: Gus's petition or Marin's «Le Mistral à nous».
  - `s1.marchand_invited` decides whether Marchand is at the door.
  - `s1.letter_trusted_to` decides who stands next to you in P9: the person you trusted in T1.

**Note (intent).** The page runs from warmth to a single photograph. The learner is not a bystander to the reveal. They are asked to *read the evidence* (L'enquête), so they are the one who places the fire upstairs. The villain the group needed, Marchand, is taken away from them in the same instant. Margaux says almost nothing, and what she says must land like her usual one perfect sentence, except that this one costs her. There is no shouting. The worst thing Gus does is drop his index cards.

**P6.** *(silence: the only text is Odile's handwriting)* *Visual:* The Polaroid alone fills the panel. A small kitchen at night. A pan on a gas ring, black smoke under the ceiling, orange light. **P7.** *Visual:* Three faces in a strip. Marin's smile going out. Gus, still on the chair, glass raised, the speech dying in his mouth. Romy's eyes moving to her camera, whose red light is still on.
- MARIN *(very low)* · A2 «Le 14 mars… C'est la nuit du feu.»
- GUS · A2 «Et alors ? Le feu, c'était en bas. Dans l'arrière-salle. Les fils électriques.» · B1 «Et alors ? Le feu est parti de l'arrière-salle, des vieux fils électriques. C'est dans le rapport. Tout le monde le sait.»

### Solve · L'enquête *(the page pauses)*

The photograph is shown again, full screen, with one prompt: **«Le feu : en bas, ou en haut ? Montre.»** («Where was the fire? Show me.»)

- **A1/A2.** Tap the detail that shows it. The target is the reversed neon, «LARTSIM». The pan and the smoke also count as «a reading»: the scene accepts the tap and Lila points to the neon.
- **B1.** Two lines appear. Tap the one the photograph contradicts: Gus's «Le feu est parti de l'arrière-salle», or the handwriting «14 mars. 23 h.». The answer is Gus's line.
- **If the learner taps elsewhere,** Lila says quietly: A2 «Là. Les lettres. Elles sont à l'envers.» · B1 «Regarde les lettres du néon. On les voit d'en haut.» The page continues. Nothing is lost.

> **Note.** This is the cruellest choice in the season, and it is deliberate. The learner is the one who reads the evidence against their own grandmother. We never praise the right answer: the scene simply moves to P8.

**P8.** *Visual:* Margaux puts down the glass she was drying. Her hands are empty for the first time in the season. She is looking at you, not at Gus. Behind her, the Polaroid from P3 leans against the till.
- MARGAUX · A2 «Non. Le feu a commencé en haut. Chez Odile.» *(beat)* «Je le sais depuis le début.» · B1 «Non. C'est parti de chez Odile.» *(beat)* «Je le sais depuis le premier soir.»

## The turn

**P9.** *Visual:* Gus climbs down from the chair too fast, and his index cards spill across the floor. He stops in front of you. On your side stands the person you trusted with the notary's letter in T1 (`s1.letter_trusted_to`; if that was nobody, Lila). The crowd is a blur of faces. Your panel is drawn from behind your shoulder, facing Gus, with an empty balloon.
- GUS · A2 «Et toi ? Tu as mis cette photo au mur. Tu le savais ?» · B1 «Et toi ? C'est toi qui as apporté ces photos. Tu le savais ?»

**Your task (what you must want to say).** In front of everyone, answer the accusation. Did you know? And say what you need to say about your grandmother, or to Margaux, who lied for her. There is no target structure. The grader listens for three things:
1. The truth about what you knew.
2. A stance towards Odile: defending her, accepting what she did, or saying you don't know.
3. Whether you acknowledge Margaux.

### Exchange 1: likely replies

**(a) Denial and defence.** *«Non ! Je ne savais pas. Je n'ai pas lu derrière. Elle était malade ?»*
- GUS *(deflating)* · A2 «D'accord. D'accord. Je te crois.» *(he turns to the zinc)* «Trois ans, Margaux. Trois ans.» · B1 «D'accord. Je te crois. C'est presque pire.» *(to the zinc)* «Trois ans, Margaux.»
- **Note.** Believing you leaves Gus with nobody to blame except the two women he loved. The scene moves to Margaux.

**(b) Straight to Margaux.** *«Margaux… Pardon. Et merci. Pourquoi tu n'as rien dit ?»*
- MARGAUX · A2 «Parce qu'elle me l'a demandé.» · B1 «Parce qu'elle me l'a demandé. Et que je le lui avais promis.»
- **Note.** No speech about loyalty. That one line carries it.

**(c) Clumsy but sincere.** *«Je sais pas… non. Ma grand-mère… elle oublie. Oubliait. Pardon. Pardon, Margaux.»*
- *(silence panel)* Margaux looks at you for a long moment. Then she lifts the hinged flap of the zinc, the barrier no customer has crossed all season.
- MARGAUX · A2 «"Pardon", c'est assez. Viens.» · B1 «"Pardon", ça suffit. Viens par là.»
- **Note.** This is the best route through the scene, and the least grammatical one. The broken tense («elle oublie… oubliait») is Odile's illness, spoken by her grandchild, and the correction belongs in the margin. From here, exchange 2 happens behind the counter, a place the learner has never been.

**(d) The accusation turned around.** *«Et toi, Margaux ? Tu as menti à tout le monde !»*
- MARGAUX · A2 «Oui.» *(she picks up a glass again)* «Pour elle. Pas pour moi.»
- **Note.** A hard route. Exchange 2 then begins with Margaux at a distance and becomes a way back. Nobody punishes the learner for anger.

### Exchange 2: Margaux tells the night *(behind or across the zinc)*

Two small flashback panels in faded inks, 2022: Odile on the stairs in her dressing gown, holding the smoking pan by its handle; Margaux in her apron with a fire extinguisher.
- ODILE *(flashback)* · A2 «Margaux… j'ai oublié la poêle.» · then: A2 «Ne dis rien. Sinon, ils vont me prendre ma maison.»
- MARGAUX *(now)* · A2 «Un mois après, ta famille est venue. Elle m'a dit au revoir comme à une cliente.» · B1 «Un mois plus tard, ta famille est venue la chercher. Elle m'a dit au revoir comme on salue une cliente.»
- *(silence panel: Margaux dries a glass that is already dry.)*
- MARGAUX · A2 «Tu m'en veux ?» · B1 «Tu m'en veux, d'avoir menti pour elle ?»

**Your task.** Tell her whether you're angry with her, and what you think of what she did.

- *«Non. Tu l'as protégée.»* → MARGAUX · A2 «Je l'ai protégée d'un feu. Pas du reste.» · B1 «Je l'ai protégée d'un feu. Pas de ce qui venait après.»
- *«Un peu. Il fallait le dire.»* → MARGAUX · A2 «Peut-être. Mais c'était sa phrase. Pas la mienne.»
- *«Je ne sais pas.»* → MARGAUX · A2 «C'est honnête. Moi non plus.» *(small smile at the corner of the mouth)*
- *Clumsy («Pas fâché… fâchée… Je suis triste pour vous deux.»)* → MARGAUX · A2 «Pour nous deux. Oui. C'est ça.»

### Exchange 3: variant by `s1.marchand_invited`

**If Marchand is here.** *Visual:* Marchand has crossed the room. He stands at the wall with his glasses lowered, reading «J'ai oublié. Encore.» The bottle in paper hangs forgotten from his hand. He comes to you and speaks with *vous*, as always.
- MARCHAND · A2 «Elle oubliait… Elle ne m'a rien dit. Moi, j'ai pensé : elle part à cause de moi.» · B1 «Elle oubliait. Et elle ne m'a rien dit. Pendant trois ans, j'ai cru qu'elle était partie à cause de moi.»
- MARCHAND · A2 «Là-bas… Elle a parlé de moi ?» · B1 «Là-bas… Est-ce qu'elle a parlé de moi ?»

**Your task.** Answer an old man's question about the woman he loved. You may tell the truth or be kind, or try to be both.
- *Truth («Non. Elle ne parlait jamais de Paris.»)* → MARCHAND · A2 «Non. Bien sûr.» *(he puts his hat back on)*
- *A kind lie («Oui. Elle parlait de vous.»)* → MARCHAND looks at you for a long time. A2 «Vous mentez gentiment. Comme Margaux.» Sets `s1.lied_to_marchand`. In T6 he remembers, without bitterness.
- *Both («Elle ne parlait pas de Paris. Mais elle a gardé une photo de vous. Je l'ai vue.»,* possible only if the learner found the Sunday-lunch Polaroid in the generated days) → MARCHAND · A2 «…Laquelle ?» *(silence panel)* He does not ask again.
- **In every route, at the door,** he converts grief into procedure. MARCHAND · A2 «Le feu est parti de chez elle. Alors les dégâts… c'est la famille qui paie. C'est vous, maintenant.» · B1 «Vous comprenez ce que ça veut dire ? Le feu est parti de chez elle. Les dégâts, c'est sa succession qui les doit. C'est-à-dire vous.»

**If Marchand is not here.** *Visual:* Romy switches the camera off in front of you, so you can see her do it.
- ROMY · A2 «J'ai éteint. Mais j'ai tout vu.» · then A2 «Tu comprends ? Si le feu vient de chez elle… les dégâts, c'est pour toi. Pour l'appartement.» · B1 «Tu comprends ce que ça implique ? Si le feu est parti de chez elle, les dégâts reviennent à sa succession. À toi.»
- ROMY · A2 «Tu veux que j'oublie ?»
- *«Oui, s'il te plaît.»* → ROMY · A2 «Je peux me taire. Je ne peux pas oublier. C'est mon métier.» · B1 «Me taire, je peux. Oublier, non. C'est mon métier, voyons.»
- *«Non. Écris la vérité.»* → ROMY · A2 «Pas ce soir. Ce soir, je suis ton amie. Demain, on verra.»
- *Clumsy («Oublier… comme elle ? Non. Pas oublier.»)* → ROMY *(a beat; she puts the lens cap on)* · A2 «D'accord. Personne n'oublie. Mais personne n'écrit. Pas encore.»

> **Note.** Both routes deliver the same blow, «your inheritance is part of the problem». Only the messenger differs, and the messenger shapes T6: the man who could forgive, or the journalist who could publish.

## Solve · Le choix

**P10.** *Visual:* The Polaroid lies on the zinc between three hands. Margaux's hand is flat, palm up. Lila's arms are folded. Your hand is nearest.
- MARGAUX · A2 «Donne. Je la range. Personne n'a besoin de savoir.»
- LILA · A2 «Tout le monde l'a vue, Margaux.» *(to you)* «Remets-la au mur.» · B1 «Ils l'ont tous vue, Margaux. La cacher, c'est mentir une deuxième fois.»

Two cards:
- **[ La rendre à Margaux ]**, subtitle «Margaux s'en souviendra. Lila aussi.» → `s1.fire_photo = margaux`. The secret continues, and it now asks thirty neighbours, and Lila, to keep it.
- **[ La remettre au mur ]**, subtitle «Lundi, tout le quartier le saura.» → `s1.fire_photo = wall`. The truth is public. So are Margaux's false declaration and Odile's illness, the dignity Margaux protected for three years.

> **Note.** Neither card is right. Giving it to Margaux is loyalty, and it makes the learner complicit. Putting it back is honesty, and it exposes both women. This choice decides whether Lila tells you about Berlin in T5 or hides it the way you hid this.

## Resolution

**R1.** *Visual:* Later. The chairs are up on the tables. The banner has half come unstuck and now reads «ON RES…». Gus sits under it counting the cagnotte jar coin by coin. Marin sweeps around him.
- GUS · A2 «Quarante-deux euros. Et un ticket de métro.» · B1 «Quarante-deux euros et un ticket de métro. Le quartier nous aime, mais avec modération.»
- MARIN · «C'est un bon ticket. Il est pas composté.»

**R2.** *(silence)* Variant by `s1.promised_to`:
- **gus:** Gus picks his index cards up off the floor one by one, squares them, and puts them in his breast pocket behind the red square. The top card says «LA PÉTITION».
- **marin:** Marin drops the «LE MISTRAL À NOUS» folder into the bin. Next panel, same frame: he takes it back out and wipes it on his sweater.
- **both_half:** Both of them, at opposite ends of the room, not looking at you.

**R3.** Variant by the choice:
- **wall:** *(silence)* The burnt Polaroid is pinned alone in the middle of a gap, where Lila has moved the others aside to give it room. Margaux, coat on, stops in front of it, then switches off the light.
- **margaux:** The till drawer. The Polaroid slides under the tray, next to the old photograph of the bar. Margaux turns the key. Lila watches with her arms folded.
  - LILA · A2 «Encore un secret. Super.» · B1 «Génial. Un secret de plus dans ce bar.»

**R4.** *Visual:* The stairwell up to Odile's floor. The timed light clicks off, then on again. You and Lila sit on the steps, a paper plate of leftover buffet between you. *(silence)*

**R5.** *Visual:* The same framing. Lila, not looking at you.
- LILA · A2 «Elle t'a laissé l'appartement… et le feu avec.» · B1 «Elle t'a laissé l'appartement. Et l'incendie avec, apparemment.»
- Her phone lights up on the step between you. She turns it face down before you can read it. **The reader can:** «Künstlerhaus Berlin — Bitte bestätigen Sie bis 15.12. / Please confirm by 15 Dec.»
> **Note.** This is the dramatic irony that launches T5. The reader knows and the learner's character does not. On the romance path, the art holds on her hand resting on the phone a beat longer.

## Agreement notes (Toi)

- **In your reply.** Learners may write «Je suis désolé / désolée», «fâché / fâchée», «sûr / sûre». Every form is accepted and none is corrected in the plot. The margin notes the agreement only if the learner has set a gender in settings.
- **Lines addressed to you.** They avoid agreement altogether: «Tu le savais ?», «Tu m'en veux ?», «Tu comprends ?». This is deliberate, and the generated days between tentpoles should keep to it.

## 1. The central question

> **Ta grand-mère t'a laissé ce qu'il y a au-dessus du Mistral, et le feu qu'il y a eu dessous. Tu le gardes, tu le partages, ou tu le laisses partir ?**
>
> Your grandmother left you what is above Le Mistral, and the fire beneath it. Will you keep it, share it, or let it go?

Each of the three endings answers the question, and each answer belongs to a friend:

- **Garder** is Gus's answer: keep everything as it was.
- **Partager** is Marin's: change so that it survives.
- **Laisser partir** is Lila's: let go, so that nobody holds anybody back.

**Logline.** You come to Paris for a week, to sign the papers for a small flat your grandmother left you and to go home. The flat is above a café called Le Mistral. Its key is on a key ring the café's owner recognises. Everyone downstairs remembers your grandmother, and each of them remembers someone different. Her last letter is addressed «Pour toi. Quand tu parleras français.»

## 2. Odile: what really happened

**Odile Ferrand (1948–2026).**

| When | What happened |
|---|---|
| **1951** | Her parents open Le Mistral on the corner of the quai de Valmy. Her father is from Provence and names the café after the wind. Odile grows up in the flat on the **first floor**, above the café. |
| **1970** | At 22 she leaves Paris for love, for «là-bas». The country is never named, so it works for any learner. Before she goes, a young painter who signs «L.» gives her a small oil of the café in the wind, painted from her window. She leaves it above the zinc. She never speaks French to her children: «Paris était trop grand pour l'autre langue.» |
| **2009** | Widowed, she comes back at 61. Her parents sold the building long ago (Marchand bought it in 1985). She buys back the one flat she can afford, her childhood flat on the first floor. She drinks a café crème at the zinc every morning. Gus's mother, Sylvie, waits tables there for Margaux (2003–2012), so Odile knows Gus from the age of 15. Odile photographs everything with an old Polaroid and writes on the back of each photo. |
| **2017–2023** | On Sundays she has lunch with M. Marchand, a widower. They take the 46 bus to the Bois de Vincennes. Nobody knows except Margaux. |
| **2019** | In a notebook she drafts «Le Mistral à nous»: the regulars would buy the walls together. Forty people pledge, among them «M. M. : oui, si Margaux reste.» |
| **2022** | She forgets to renew her home insurance. |
| **13 March 2023** | Marchand asks her to come and live with him on the fourth floor. She says «Je vais réfléchir.» In her notebook she writes: «Je ne peux pas lui dire que j'oublie.» |
| **14 March 2023, 23:00** | She forgets a pan on the stove. She photographs the smoke («J'ai oublié. Encore.»), comes down in her dressing gown and fetches Margaux, who puts it out with the café's extinguisher. Odile says: «Ne dis rien. Sinon, ils vont me prendre ma maison.» At **1:30**, after closing, embers that fell into the old shared ventilation duct set fire to the café's back room, where the electrical board is. Margaux tells the firemen and the insurers that nothing unusual happened that night. The next week she repaints the wall above Odile's stove. |
| **April 2023** | Odile avoids Marchand. Her family comes to fetch her. She says goodbye to Margaux as if Margaux were a customer. Nobody opens the flat again. The family pays the charges and never comes back. |
| **2026** | Odile dies and leaves the flat to her grandchild, the learner. The notary calls it «modeste». |

### How she created the dispute

The building insurer's expert blames the café's electrical board. Margaux's own insurer finds signs that the fire came *down* the duct, sees that this does not match her declaration, stops paying and asks for the advance back. Margaux falls behind on the rent. Marchand owns the walls and the floors above. He is 74, cannot climb to his fourth-floor flat any more (he sleeps on a camp bed in his office), and has an offer from **Solvel Immobilier** for the whole building. He refuses to renew Margaux's lease *pour motif grave*, her «negligence», which would save him the eviction compensation. The building has one floor he does not own: **the first floor, yours.**

**What the truth does.** It clears Margaux of negligence, so Marchand's *motif grave* collapses. But it exposes her false declaration, and it makes Odile's estate (the learner) liable for the damage. Odile's insurance had lapsed, so the estate pays. The notary advises accepting the estate *à concurrence de l'actif net*, which means the debt can take the flat but no more than the flat.

### Plausibility read (S-6: for a French reader, not a lawyer)

These are the points a reader should check:

1. A pan fire at 23:00 smouldering in a shared duct until 1:30 in an old Paris building.
2. An insurer contesting a claim over an inconsistent declaration.
3. A *bail commercial* refused *pour motif grave* (no *indemnité d'éviction*), where that motive falls if the cause lies elsewhere.
4. An heir's liability for a fire started by the deceased (civil fault, no insurance), limited by accepting the estate *à concurrence de l'actif net*.
5. A developer buying a building floor by floor.

None of this is explained on screen beyond one or two A2 lines per tentpole. The page says only what the characters would say: «Le feu a commencé en haut», «Les dégâts, c'est pour la famille», «Il ne renouvelle pas le bail».

## 4. The cast bible

**Register rules.** Every speech sample is given at **A2** (short, common words, present / passé composé / futur proche) and at **B1** (imparfait, conditionnel, relative clauses, the odd subjunctive). All lines addressed to Toi, and all of Camille's lines about Camille, are **agreement-free** (see §9).

### Toi (the learner)
- **Situation.** Arrives from «là-bas» with a suitcase, the notary's envelope and a key on Odile's key ring, which has a small enamel **cicada** (Provence). Booked a return ticket for a week later. Rents the short-let studio with the cold radiator (canon).
- **Want.** To sign and leave. Then to know who she was. Then, without admitting it, to stay.
- **Fear.** Being the stranger who inherits a life that belongs to other people.
- **Secret.** You barely knew her. The last time you saw her she called you by your parent's name.
- **Nicknames.** Lila calls you **«l'héritage»** («Salut, l'héritage !»). Marchand says **«la succession Ferrand»**. Both are epicene, and both are running gags.
- **Small moments.** Your usual order becomes known (`user.usual_order`). You move your return ticket, again (`user.return_ticket`). You fix the cicada back onto the ring.
- **Visual rule.** Never shown face-on. From behind, cropped, or reflected in a dark window. The slightly-too-heavy coat.
  - **Owner, 2026-10-02:** this rule is replaced. The learner makes their own face at registration (WP-118 «Mon personnage»), and Toi is shown face-on like the cast. The heavy coat stays. Open: Lila's portrait beat («never a face», T5/T8) needs a new payoff.

### Lila Bonnet (tu)
- **Role.** Teaches a CE1 class (she has Wednesdays off) and paints. Marin's flatmate and oldest friend. They were together for two months in 2016 and still laugh about it (S-1). She becomes your accomplice upstairs.
- **Want.** Berlin: a one-year residency at the (fictional) Künstlerhaus am Kanal. She has already turned down one, in 2021.
- **Fear.** That leaving means she never loved them. That staying means she never loved herself.
- **Secret.** The 2026 offer, due 15 December. Odile knew about the 2021 one («Lila. Elle part ou elle reste ?»).
- **How she speaks.**
  - A2: «Mais arrête. C'est évident.» · «Toi, tu me caches quelque chose.» · «Bon, on fait quoi ?»
  - B1: «Tu vas pas me faire croire que c'est un hasard.» · «Je déteste avoir raison la nuit.»
  - Marseille exclamations when caught off guard («Oh, fan de chichourle !»).
- **Running gags.**
  - Grades everyone's life decisions like homework: «Sept sur vingt. Peut mieux faire.»
  - Says «la lumière est fausse» about the painting above the zinc.
  - Mocks your «vous»: «Tu me vouvoies ? Je le dis à ma classe.»
- **Small moments.** Remembers your usual order before Margaux does. Paint on her thumb. The ochre scarf. Draws on napkins while others argue. The corner of her portrait slowly gains you, from behind.

### Marin Lévêque (tu)
- **Role.** Works at an environmental NGO and writes a novel nobody may read. The co-op plan **«Le Mistral à nous»** replaces his ring proposal (S-2). The idea comes from his father's fishermen's co-operative in Finistère, and Odile's notebook proves he isn't the first to have it.
- **Want.** That the café survives, even changed. That something he builds holds people together.
- **Fear.** Being the one who breaks the group. His father's «un bureau à Paris, c'est pas un métier».
- **Secret.** He has already put €4,000 of savings in as the first share.
- **How he speaks.**
  - A2: «C'est un signe, ça.» · «Bon. Qui veut une soupe ?» · «Ma grand-mère disait toujours…»
  - B1: «Si ça, c'est pas un signe, je ne sais pas ce qu'il te faut.»
- **Running gags.**
  - His grandmother's sayings grow more absurd: «Ma grand-mère disait toujours : quand la mouette est sur le clocher, on ne vend pas sa maison.»
  - Cries at adverts.
  - Feeds everyone (far breton, soupe de poisson).
  - Breton words, which he then explains («Kenavo ! Ça veut dire au revoir. Enfin, à la prochaine.»).
- **Small moments.** Brings soup when you're ill. Keeps his father's voicemails. Holds the chair, and later the ladder.

### Augustin «Gus» de Roncourt (tu with the group; theatrical vous as a weapon)
- **Role.** Consultant («du conseil»), showman, inventor of «La Méthode». Wants Le Mistral kept *exactly* as it is: the zinc, the booth, the plaque. He says his great-grandfather laid the zinc.
- **Want.** To keep the booth where he did his homework while his mother worked the room, without ever having to say that sentence.
- **Fear.** Being seen: Créteil, his mother, the boy who said he'd be a count.
- **Secret.** «De Roncourt» was invented at 15, and Odile's Polaroid was there («Augustin, 15 ans. Il dit qu'il sera comte.»). He pays other people's tabs anonymously.
- **How he speaks.**
  - A2: «Règle numéro un de La Méthode : …» · «Mesdames, messieurs !» · «C'est magnifique. Non : c'est sublime.»
  - B1: «Costume… attends… né pour ça.» · «Je réclame votre silence ou, à défaut, votre indulgence.»
- **Running gags.**
  - Index cards for every speech. They fall.
  - Superlatives that grow.
  - «Au château» on Sundays.
  - Uses *vous* on people he dislikes, and on you until T3.
- **Small moments.** Always sits in the booth's corner seat: his mother's break table. Straightens the salt cellars when upset.

### Margaux (tu)
- **Role.** Owner-bartender of Le Mistral since 2001, in her late fifties. The oracle.
- **Want.** To keep her promise to Odile, and the café, and never to ask anyone for help.
- **Fear.** Saying one sentence too many.
- **Secret.** The fire (§2). The Polaroid of Odile behind the till.
- **How she speaks.** A2: «La même chose ?» · «J'ai rien entendu.» · «Bois.» · B1: «Ça finira bien, ou ça finira. Bois.» She uses the fewest words in the cast. When she says two sentences in a row, something is wrong.
- **Running gags.** Dries a glass that is already dry. «J'ai rien entendu» after hearing everything. Bans people from her zinc.
- **Small moments.** Keeps the booth. Pours a café crème nobody ordered, for Odile's stool. Lifts the zinc's hinged flap for you once (T4).

### Romy Tremblay (tu)
- **Role.** Québécoise journalist; your **friend** (S-2; the romance is set aside). She is investigating Solvel's purchases of cafés in the 10th and 11th, «les bistrots qui disparaissent». **Her camera recorded the reveal in T4:** the memory card, `romy.footage`, is a thread to the finale.
- **Want.** The story that earns her a Paris contract, earned by listening, not by leaving.
- **Fear.** Being the one who publishes the thing that breaks her friends.
- **Secret.** She kept her Montréal apartment (canon), and she kept the card.
- **How she speaks.** A2: «Voyons donc.» · «C'est quoi, la vraie histoire ?» · «C'est pas pire.» · B1: «Je capote.» · «Me taire, je peux. Oublier, non.»
- **Running gags.** Québec against Paris words: *souper / dîner*, *char / voiture*, *magasiner*. The Réveillon menu becomes a war. Performs *vous* badly on purpose. The red light of her camera.
- **Small moments.** Switches the camera off in front of you, so you can see her do it. Brings you the one Québec thing that helps (a tuque in December).

### M. Marchand (vous)
- **Role.** Owns the building (the café's walls and floors 2–4) and runs his other two buildings from a 2nd-floor office. He is 74 and widowed. He has not reached his 4th-floor flat for months, and sleeps on a camp bed among the files. **The opponent who becomes the other side** (T6).
- **Want.** A home without stairs, not to be a burden to Camille, and to know why Odile left.
- **Fear.** That she left because of his question.
- **Secret.** The Sundays with Odile. His pledge «oui, si Margaux reste».
- **How he speaks.** A2: «La succession Ferrand est en retard.» · «C'est mon immeuble.» · B1: «Je vous rappelle que le bail expire le 31 mars.» He writes *recommandé avec accusé de réception* letters. He turns grief into procedure.
- **Running gags.** The ring of old keys. The 46 bus. Calls the learner «la succession Ferrand» to their face.
- **Small moments.** Still buys two croissants on Sundays. Reads a Polaroid with his glasses pushed down his nose.

### Camille Marchand (vous; second romantic possibility, S-5)
- **Role.** Marchand's grandchild, about thirty, an urban planner at a consultancy. Born in the 10th and still rooted there. Handles the sale for their grandfather.
- **Want.** For Grand-père to be safe and for the numbers to work. «Un immeuble, ce n'est pas un musée.»
- **Fear.** Being the family member who sold the family.
- **Secret.** Camille loved Le Mistral as a child: hot chocolate on Wednesdays, before the family fell out with the café.
- **How Camille speaks.** A2: «Les chiffres sont simples.» · «Je ne suis pas d'accord.» · B1: «Je comprends. Je ne suis toujours pas d'accord.» Precise, dry, never contemptuous. Their lines never agree an adjective with themselves.
- **Running gags.** Arrives everywhere by bike, helmet under one arm, slightly rained on. Corrects everyone's numbers, including Gus's superlatives.
- **Small moments.** Carries Grand-père's shopping up. Knows the year of every building on the quai.
- **Gender.** Set by the learner in T1 Day B (§9). The art has two descriptors (see `10`), and other characters' pronouns come from the flag.
- **Arc.** Attraction grows through disagreement: T1 meeting, T2 visit, T6 argument, T8 «À la prochaine dispute.». The romance belongs to season 2. In season 1, Camille stays on *vous*.

### «L.» (season-2 seed, S-4)
- The man Odile left in 1970, a painter, now about 80, possibly alive on rue de Lancry, 200 m from the café.
- **In season 1** he appears only as:
  - the signature on the painting;
  - an old man at a third-floor window with an easel, glimpsed in the background of three panels (T1 Day A P1, T5 A R1, T8 Day B);
  - the back of the painting in T8.
- Nobody names him, and nobody notices him. The reader might.

### Minor characters
- **Mme Diallo,** the baker, with her running line «Une tradition pas trop cuite, c'est ça ?».
- **Maître Vasseur,** the notary. Brisk and kind.
- **Bastien Roux,** Solvel's acquisitions man, in a good coat (canon s3). He measures rooms.

## 5. The three arcs

### A · The key and the café (mystery and belonging)
- **Question.** Who was Odile, and why did she leave?
- **Spine.**
  - **T1:** you arrive and are taken for the seller.
  - **T2:** upstairs, the calendar shows she had not planned to leave.
  - **T3:** her notebook plan gets two claimants.
  - **T4:** the fire was hers, and Margaux lied for her.
  - **T6:** her last pages show why she left (the question she could not answer, and her memory).
  - **T8:** her letter, and «quelqu'un».
- **The learner's change.** From heir to the one who holds her story, and decides who gets it.

### B · Lila and Berlin (slow romance, or deep friendship)
- **Question.** Can you want someone here and want their leaving for them?
- **Spine.**
  - **T1:** she laughs at the mistake.
  - **T2:** the balcony, and «Elle part ou elle reste ?».
  - **Gaps:** the dinner, the roof, the argument.
  - **T4:** her phone lights up.
  - **T5:** Berlin.
  - **T6:** the one o'clock phone call.
  - **T7:** midnight.
  - **T8:** the platform.
- **Rule.** Lila never stays (S-3). Her future is hers.

### C · The future of Le Mistral (friendship and rivalry)
- **Question.** Keep it as it was, change it, or let it go?
- **Spine.**
  - **T1:** Gus's weaponised *vous*.
  - **T3:** two promises, and Marchand invited.
  - **T4:** the villain is taken away.
  - **T5:** Marin's plan needs Lila.
  - **T6:** Marchand's account and the notary at 10:00.
  - **T7:** the plan your choices allow.
  - **T8:** the ending.
- **Rule.** Every position is defensible. Nobody is wrong for wanting their ending.

### Where they collide
- **The upstairs box** supports Marin (Odile's co-op) and undermines Gus (the count at 15). **T2–T3.**
- **Lila helps you reveal the fire** (she says «remets-la au mur»), then resents keeping secrets. **T4–T5.**
- **Marin's plan** is built on the workshop of the friend who is leaving. **T5.**
- **The notebook** heals Marchand and can ruin Margaux. **T6.**
- **Solvel's offer and Lila's train** expire on the same day, 8 January. **T8.**

## 7. Lila: the romance path and the friendship path

**The rule.** Both paths share the same events. What changes is what Lila's lines carry, and a handful of gestures. The path moves only on **what the learner chooses to express** at five gates. It never depends on accuracy, band or length. A clumsy sentence that means «j'aime être avec toi» moves it. A perfect sentence that means «bonne nuit» does not.

| Gate | The moment | What moves toward romance | What moves toward friendship |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · T2 B | The balcony: «Tu restes combien de temps, en fait ?» | Staying *because of* her or this: «Je ne sais pas. Moins, avant de te rencontrer.» | Staying because of the flat or the mystery, or a joke |
| 2 · Gap 2 | The disastrous improvised dinner, which you save with one line | Tenderness toward her: «C'est le meilleur dîner raté de ma vie.» | Complicity: «On dit à Marin que c'était exprès.» |
| 3 · Gap 4 | After the argument («encore un secret»), a hesitant apology | Naming what she means to you: «Je ne veux pas me disputer avec toi. Pas toi.» | Naming the alliance: «On est une équipe. Pardon.» |
| 4 · T5 | «Dis-moi un truc vrai.» | «Je te veux ici», even as the clumsy «je veux toi ici» | «Je veux ça pour toi», or honest support |
| 5 · T7 A | Midnight on the pavement | A confession, however clumsy | A pact |

- **How the path is decided.** Two romance-leaning expressions make the path romance. A single one leaves it *open*: Lila notices, and a small moment registers it. The learner can steer back at any time, by naming friendship or by letting a romantic opening pass. **Nothing is ever lost:** the friendship path has its own best scenes.
- **How affection shows (never as a score).**
  - Lila remembers your usual order.
  - She saves the chair next to her.
  - Her teasing about your *vous* stops.
  - An unfinished text: «Tu dors ?», deleted, seen in the art.
  - The corner figure in her portrait gains your coat, then your hands, then almost a face.
  - On the romance path, her thumb lingers when she wipes paint off your hand.
- **Romance beats.**
  - Restraint first: «Pas maintenant. Mais pas jamais.» (T5).
  - A kiss only in T7 A, and only after the learner has said something true. Lila moves last, never first.

## 9. Writing rules for this season

- **Toi is gender-neutral.** Lines *addressed to* Toi avoid agreement: «Tu le savais ?», «Tu es en colère ?», «la première personne». The learner's own lines may agree freely, and every form is accepted. The margin notes agreement only if the learner has set a gender in settings.
- **Camille (S-5).** In T1 Day B, just before Camille's first close-up, a quiet card outside the fiction asks «Camille, pour toi, c'est… [un homme] [une femme]». Camille's own lines are agreement-free. Other characters use «il» or «elle» from the flag, and the script writes these as «il/elle». Owner option: take the default from the learner's settings instead.
- **Silence is a tool.** Every tentpole day has at least one panel without dialogue.
- **Nobody explains their feelings.** People act (they dry a dry glass, lift the flap of the zinc, turn a phone face down) and say one sentence.
- **Humour in every tentpole day.** It comes from Gus and Marin first, then Romy's words, Lila's grades and Marchand's procedure.
- **Grammar is corrected in the margin, never in the plot.** The clumsy, sincere reply must always be able to lead to the best version of a scene.
- **Every episode changes something (§ `09`).** Episodes without meaningful change are refused.
- **The weight is accepted (S-8).** Odile's memory and the fire's debt are real. They are never played for pity and never solved by a speech.

# L'escalier du Mistral: the stairwell and the door with the «1»

The first room the forge writes from the canon instead of a map survey, and the proof of the
room kit (`room.ts`, `room.py`). It stands inside Le Mistral's building, where the quai's façade
has its street door (bay 3, z −21.65 to −19.1): the vestibule behind that door, a corridor along
the courtyard side, one flight up along the party wall, the first-floor landing, and Odile's door
in its wall. Her kitchen is behind it, seen and never entered: its window is the one in the
quai's façade, bay 2. The place stays `locked` in `world.json`; the story opens it.

## What the canon gives

From `docs/story/season-1/10-lieux-et-images.md`:

- **The stairwell.** «A narrow old Paris stairwell with worn wooden steps, a stair window onto the
  courtyard, and a push-button timed light (*minuterie*) that clicks off mid-scene. On the
  first-floor door there is a brass numeral «1».» And: «The light going out is a punctuation
  mark.»
- **Odile's flat, T1.** «The door is opened and closed: darkness, street light through the slats,
  a stopped kitchen clock. We see only the room and your hand.» With the flat's descriptor: the
  shutters closed, the green coat on a hook by the door, the slippers side by side under a chair,
  the Polaroid camera on the table beside a cup, the calendar showing April 2023, the whiter
  patch above the gas stove. «Never mess, decay or horror. It is a room that waited, not a room
  that rotted.»
- **T1 R1 to R3** (the scene file): after closing, one flight up, the timed light ticking; the key
  turns and the door opens onto darkness; the same framing, the door closing again: you have not
  gone in. Nobody goes in before T2.

The forge's own choices, not canon: the hour the clock stopped at (3:40), the plan (a straight
flight beside a corridor, which the walking grid can hold), the 50 seconds of the minuterie. The
letterboxes carry only the canon's names («O. Ferrand» on the first floor, «Gérance Marchand» on
the second, «Marchand» on the fourth, from the bible and the places file); the other floors'
boxes are blank rather than invent tenants.

## How it plays

You come in from the street in the dark. The orange push-button by the door glows; a press lights
the stairs (the globe in the vestibule, the sconce at the foot, the round lamp on the landing) and
the clockwork ticks. Climb; the door with the «1» is next. A tap and the key turns, the door swings
onto the dark kitchen, and you look in from the threshold: the coat, the slatted light, the
calendar, the clock (R2). It shuts again (R3), and soon after the light goes out on it. The door
the story opens once stays shut on a later visit («Pas avant mercredi»). The journal keeps what
you did.

## Round 1 (r1, 844×844, load 0.27 s, 10k to 11k triangles, 13 views)

- **What reads.** The flight from its foot and from its top: worn treads, the balustrade's iron
  bars and wooden rail, the dado following the slope, the corridor's pale floor beyond. The
  kitchen through the open door: the slats lit by the street, the calendar «AVRIL 2023», the
  stopped clock, a bar of light on the floor.
- **The coat** read as a hooded figure in the dark: a round body with a domed top. That is the
  horror the canon forbids. It is now a coat on a hook: hung from its loop, the shoulders falling
  away, sleeves, lapels, three buttons, pockets, flat against the wall, in a green that stays
  green at night.
- **An empty shaft.** The cage rose 6 m above the landing to its ceiling. The walking grid is one
  sheet, so no flight can climb on over the corridor (the landing it starts from would hang over
  cells you walk below). The cage now closes at 8.4 m, a storey above the landing.
- **The letterboxes** had «2e · Gérance Marchand». Read against the bible alone (Marchand's flat is
  on the fourth floor), that looked wrong, and his name moved to the fourth. Round 3 puts it back.
- **The camera.** The door's view stood behind the landing's balustrade and fell to the corridor
  below; the plan from above saw only the ceilings. Views now step back only as far as the floor
  carries you, and a plan of each floor is cut under what is over it.

## Round 2 (r2, 14 views)

- **What the things say.** A view of the letterboxes, read from a step away: the names fit their
  cards.
- **Odile's door** was a flat slab with two bars across it and a «1» too thin to read. The panels
  now have mouldings all round, each printing its own outline, and the «1» is 17 cm of brass at
  eye height. The stage stands you 0.8 m from it, looking between the knob and the number.

## Round 3 (r3, 14 views)

- **A gap round the leaf** showed blue at both jambs: the leaf hangs in the depth of the wall and
  stopped short of it. A door now has its stop on the side it does not swing to.
- **«Gérance Marchand» is canon**: the places file puts Marchand's office on the second floor. It
  is back on the second letterbox, and his name stays on the fourth (his flat, which he can no
  longer climb to).
- Looked at again: the door with its «1», the coat, the letterboxes, both plans. Nothing else
  changed.

## Played (the standalone page, 390×844, `?place=escalier_mistral&debug`)

The whole of it, with taps where a player taps: the dark arrival, the push-button tapped where it
glows, «Y aller» up the flight, the door, R2, R3, the light going out, a second tap on the door
(«La porte reste fermée. Pas avant mercredi.»), «Rentrer» and the end card (the three things you
did, and the door shut until Wednesday). The quarter remembers the door as opened and the place
as visited; the journal has the line. About 11k triangles, no errors. What the phone showed, and
what changed:

- **The arrival** had the push-button at the screen's edge on an upright phone: the start now
  looks toward it, and it glows left of the middle.
- **On the landing** you arrived facing the way you came, with the door behind you. You now turn
  to the door when you reach the landing, and to the button or the door when «Y aller» gets you
  there.
- **R2** lost the clock and the calendar on an upright phone (the lens of a square frame). The
  director now opens the lens until 46° of the room fit across: coat, slatted window, clock and
  calendar in one frame.
- **R3** was played from the threshold, which is where the leaf shuts: it would have closed
  through you. It is now played from where you stood before the door, looking in: the stove and
  the whiter patch above it in the slatted light, and the leaf with its «1» swinging shut.

## Still plain

- **The stairs stop at the first floor**: the walking grid is one sheet, and a flight climbing on
  over the corridor would need a landing over it. The cage closes a storey above the landing.
- **Nobody is in it yet.** Lila on the lower steps (R3, R4) is the stage's to place: the brief has
  her mark (`lilaStep`). No hand on the frame in R2.
- **The kitchen only.** The flat's second room, its balcony over the canal and the shoebox under
  the bed are T2's, behind the doorway on the kitchen's west side.
- **The tomettes** are the café's tile pattern in terracotta, square, not hexagonal.
- **The dark** is a dim grey-blue, not black, so the push-button and the way to it stay legible
  on a phone.
- **Photos.** None looked at: no open street photograph shows the inside of this building, and
  the canon is the reference.

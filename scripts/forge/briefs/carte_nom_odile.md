# La carte d'Odile: her name in Lila's hand, pinned on the wall by the corner booth

`forge.py queue --season --from api` on 22 Nov put the learners on g4 and g5 and printed `next:
none`, so the run takes the next thing the learners' own pages left, from `queue --orders`: «la
carte d'Odile» at `le_mistral` (one life). The order says: «A small card with Odile's name in
Lila's hand, pinned on the wall beside the corner booth.» It waits on that mark: a learner sees it
only once a page of their own left it there.

## What the canon gives

- **Le Mistral** (`10-lieux-et-images.md`): the ox-blood booth at the back, its corner seat Gus's.
  For the T4 party, the Polaroid wall above the booth: «rows of white-bordered squares, each with a
  small handwritten card beneath». This card is one of those cards, before the wall.
- **Lila** paints; her colour is ochre («Lila's ochre»).
- **Odile** never speaks; her name may be written.

## The forge's own choices, not canon

- **Where.** On the north wall above the booth's back, by Gus's corner seat, under the old «Bal»
  poster in its black frame and short of the corner. It faces the room.
- **The card.** Cream card stock, 15 by 10 cm, pinned through its top by a red drawing pin.
  «Odile» is written large in a painter's hand, in ochre ink gone dark, with a quick brush stroke
  under it. Nothing else on it.
- **Not the bank card.** Yesterday's «La carte bleue d'Odile» lies on the zinc; this is another
  order and another thing, and the test says so.

## The kit

- `pinned`, new in `room.ts` and in `scripts/forge/room.py`'s list (`text[0]` the words, `size`
  [width, height], `at` the wall's face at its centre).

## Rounds

1. At 11 by 7.5 cm it read as a light switch from the room, and the ochre name was faint on the
   cream.
2. A bigger card and darker ink: close up it reads «Odile» with the stroke under it. The pin hardly
   showed.
3. The pin's head bigger and a true red: the card reads as pinned.

## Seen

On the standalone page (`?detail=carte_nom_odile&debug`) at 900×600 and 390×844: from the zinc
and the door it is a small pale card under the poster, as a small card across a café is; from the
booth's table and close up, the name, the stroke and the red pin read. No errors.
